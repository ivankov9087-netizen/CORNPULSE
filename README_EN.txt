CORNPULSE v0.5 Windows x64 Edition
Created by: Fields Food

This is an offline, fan-made FPS created for viewers of my channel.
It combines movement mechanics such as portals, grappling, and wall-running
with roguelike elements that let you grow stronger as you fight hordes of enemies.
This is not an official or endorsed title from 1047 Games.
It is an unofficial fan-made project.

[1. How to Launch]
1) Right-click the downloaded ZIP file and select "Extract All...".
2) Launch "CORNPULSE.exe" inside the extracted folder.
3) Select "Deploy" on the title screen to start the main mode.

Do not run the game directly from inside the ZIP or move the EXE to another location on its own.
The included DLLs, resources, locales, and other files are required for the game to run.
This release is for 64-bit Windows. A keyboard and mouse are required.
Gamepads are not supported.

Gamepad support is possible, but I am not familiar enough with gamepads to fine-tune the controls.
Any game developers who are good with a controller, please help me out! (sob)

[2. Uninstalling]
This game does not require installation.
To remove it, close the game and delete the entire extracted game folder.
Settings, suspended-run data, caches, and other data remain in %APPDATA%\CORNPULSE.
Deleting that folder as well will remove the game's settings and saved data.
No registry changes are required to uninstall the game.

[3. If a SmartScreen Warning Appears]
The CORNPULSE.exe included in this release is unsigned. Digital code signing costs money.
The creator has no money. Sorry.

Windows SmartScreen may display "Windows protected your PC"
for unsigned apps with little distribution history.
This message alone does not mean that a virus has been detected.

Make sure you obtained the game from a download location linked in Fields Food's videos or posts.
If you choose to run it, follow these steps on the warning screen:

1) Click "More info".
2) Check that the app name is "CORNPULSE.exe".
   Because the app is unsigned, the publisher will be shown as "Unknown publisher".
3) Click "Run anyway".

You do not need to disable SmartScreen or your antivirus software.
Depending on your environment or updates, the warning may appear again.

[5. Suspending, Resuming, and PASSWORD]
To stop playing and resume another day, select "Suspend" on the death screen.
After relaunching the game, use the resume button on the title screen to continue.
Simply closing the window or selecting "Return to Title"
will not save your current RUN as a suspended run.

We recommend saving the "PASSWORD" shown after suspending a run in Notepad or a similar app.
Paste it into "PASSWORD" on the title screen to resume, even on another PC.
The PASSWORD on the clear screen lets you carry your card Ranks and COINS into the next loop.

Location of settings, suspended-run data, and other saved data:
%APPDATA%\CORNPULSE

This save folder remains even after you delete the game.
If you also delete the save folder, make sure to copy any PASSWORD you need beforehand.

[9. A Few Personal Thoughts]
CORNPULSE is my first personal game project. It started with a simple thought:
"I want to try making an FPS that I would want to play myself."
(Though even calling this "game development" feels a bit presumptuous...)

At the same time, as someone who introduces games to an audience, I wanted to experience
for myself what AI-assisted development can do, and where the difficulties lie.

AI was used extensively to implement the game,
and most of the code was generated by AI.
I prototyped it through what is often called "vibe coding," then played it myself
and decided which direction to take the revisions as development continued.

The starting point was to combine portal-based spatial manipulation and high-speed movement
from two games I love: "Splitgate" and "EMPULSE."

But once I actually tried it, unexpected ways to play began to emerge:
huge leaps created by combining portals and grappling, or opening a portal on speed paint
and launching through it with the momentum from the boost.
These were things I had not anticipated at the start.

By keeping those discoveries and refining them to make them even more fun,
the game gradually began to develop its own identity.
It was not finished with a single prompt.

I playtested repeatedly, built on the parts I enjoyed,
and changed the parts that did not work well together.
Level design and balance went through the same process,
with many rounds of prototyping, revision, and testing.

The illustration of the character Iwana-chan was commissioned from a professional illustrator.
Some of the voice lines used in the game were provided by someone with professional voice acting experience.
For the music, I used Suno Premier, then rebuilt and finished the tracks in Ableton Live.
The gun 3D models are from Quaternius' Ultimate Guns Pack (CC0), with the colors adjusted by me.
The textures were generated using ChatGPT.
I combined these materials and repeatedly playtested and adjusted the game to bring the overall experience together.

What I realized through this project is that even when AI helps you make something that runs,
it was still essential to keep asking, "What kind of gameplay am I aiming for?"
and "What do I find fun, and what should I keep?"

This does not mean I have learned to write a program from scratch.
But it gave me a chance to learn, in my own way, about the difficulties of making games
that I had not understood from the player's side.
And it made me feel even more strongly that I want to show respect for the game developers
who give so much of their lives to entertain us.

Above all, I set out to make something I would want to play myself.
It's a just-for-fun FPS made for my videos and for viewers to play.
I would be happy if fans of high-speed shooters enjoyed it too.
